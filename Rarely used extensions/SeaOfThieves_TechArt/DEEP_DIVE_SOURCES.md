## 2. Deep Dive 1: Ocean Simulation & Jacobian Whitecaps (Tessendorf 2001)

### Mathematical Formulation

The foundation of the ocean surface in *Sea of Thieves* relies on Jerry Tessendorf's statistical oceanographic synthesis, evaluated using the Fast Fourier Transform (FFT).

#### 1. The Phillips Wave Spectrum
The wave field at spatial frequency vector $\mathbf{k} = (k_x, k_z)$ is modeled using the Phillips spectrum:
$$P_h(\mathbf{k}) = A \frac{\exp\left(-\frac{1}{(k L)^2}\right)}{k^4} |\hat{\mathbf{k}} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{w}}|^2 \exp(-k^2 \ell^2)$$

Where:
- $L = \frac{V^2}{g}$ is the largest wave scale generated by wind speed $V$ under gravity $g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$.
- $\hat{\mathbf{w}}$ is the normalized wind direction vector.
- $\ell$ is a damping factor to suppress arbitrarily small high-frequency ripples (preventing aliasing).
- $A$ is an empirical wave amplitude parameter.

#### 2. Deep Water Dispersion Relation
The angular frequency $\omega(k)$ for deep ocean water waves is:
$$\omega(k) = \sqrt{g k}, \quad \text{where } k = |\mathbf{k}| = \sqrt{k_x^2 + k_z^2}$$

#### 3. Spatial Displacement Vector $\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{x}, t)$
To generate sharp, peaked crests and wide, flat troughs (trochoidal / Gerstner shapes), the surface points $\mathbf{x} = (x, z)$ are displaced horizontally as well as vertically:
$$\mathbf{x}_{\text{displaced}} = \mathbf{x} + \lambda \mathbf{D}(\mathbf{x}, t)$$
$$\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{x}, t) = \sum_{\mathbf{k}} -i \frac{\mathbf{k}}{k} \tilde{h}(\mathbf{k}, t) e^{i \mathbf{k} \cdot \mathbf{x}}$$
Where $\lambda$ governs horizontal choppiness.

#### 4. The Jacobian Determinant for Wave Crest Folding & Foam
In the presentation, Valentine Kozin highlights:
> *"You take the Jacobian determinant of the transform and generate foam where it goes negative. We inject pixels into a render target which tiles across the water surface... This is an indicator of where choppiness offsets cause the wave peaks to overlap."*

The transformation mapping undisturbed coordinate $\mathbf{x}$ to displaced coordinate $\mathbf{x}'$ is:
$$\mathbf{x}'(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{x} + \lambda \mathbf{D}(\mathbf{x})$$

The Jacobian matrix of this mapping is:
$$J(\mathbf{x}) = \nabla \mathbf{x}' = \begin{pmatrix} 1 + \lambda \frac{\partial D_x}{\partial x} & \lambda \frac{\partial D_x}{\partial z} \\ \lambda \frac{\partial D_z}{\partial x} & 1 + \lambda \frac{\partial D_z}{\partial z} \end{pmatrix}$$

The determinant is:
$$\det(J) = \left(1 + \lambda \frac{\partial D_x}{\partial x}\right)\left(1 + \lambda \frac{\partial D_z}{\partial z}\right) - \lambda^2 \frac{\partial D_x}{\partial z}\frac{\partial D_z}{\partial x}$$

**Physical Significance:**
- $\det(J) > 1$: The surface is expanding (troughs stretching out).
- $0 < \det(J) \le 1$: Moderate compression.
- $\det(J) \le 0$: The surface has compressed so severely that wave crests fold over themselves (self-intersection). In the real world, this is the exact moment water turbulence traps air and forms **whitecaps / sea foam**.

#### HLSL Shader Implementation of Jacobian Foam Generation:

```hlsl
// Evaluated on the GPU compute/pixel pass generating the foam mask
float CalculateJacobianFoam(float2 dDx_dx, float2 dDz_dz, float lambda, float foamThreshold)
{
    float Jxx = 1.0 + lambda * dDx_dx.x;
    float Jzz = 1.0 + lambda * dDz_dz.y;
    float Jxz = lambda * dDx_dx.y;
    float Jzx = lambda * dDz_dz.x;

    // Jacobian determinant
    float detJ = Jxx * Jzz - Jxz * Jzx;

    // Foam generated when determinant drops below threshold
    float foamIntensity = saturate((foamThreshold - detJ) / foamThreshold);
    return foamIntensity;
}
```

## 3. Deep Dive 2: Ship Deck Shallow Water Simulation (Mei et al. 2007)

### Citation & Context
- **Paper:** *"Fast Hydraulic Erosion Simulation and Visualization on GPU"* (Xing Mei, Philippe Decaudin, Bao-Gang Hu, *Pacific Graphics 2007*).
- **In-Engine Use:** Water crashing over the bow onto the ship deck, flowing dynamically between floorboards and draining away through scuppers in ~0.2 ms.

### The 2-Pass Pipe Model Fluid Algorithm

The simulation discretizes the ship deck into a $512 \times 512$ grid cell heightfield with cell dimensions $l_x, l_y$:
- $b(x, y)$: Terrain / deck plank height (contains crevices).
- $d(x, y)$: Water layer thickness.
- Total surface height: $\eta = b + d$.

#### Step 1: Outflow Flux Update (Pass 1)
Each cell communicates with its 4 orthogonal neighbors (Left, Right, Top, Bottom) via virtual pipes with cross-sectional area $A$. The hydrostatic pressure difference drives outflow:
$$\Delta h^L = \eta(x, y) - \eta(x - 1, y)$$
$$f_{t+\Delta t}^L = \max\left(0, f_t^L + \Delta t \cdot A \cdot \frac{g \cdot \Delta h^L}{l_x}\right)$$

If the sum of outgoing fluxes exceeds the total available water in the cell, fluxes are scaled down by a factor $K$ to ensure mass conservation:
$$K = \min\left(1, \frac{d(x, y) \cdot l_x l_y}{\sum f_{\text{out}} \cdot \Delta t}\right)$$
$$f_{\text{final}} = f \cdot K$$

#### Step 2: Water Height & Leakage Update (Pass 2)
In the second pass, water height is updated by net flux balance plus water added from bow waves minus water leaking through cracks:
$$d_{t+\Delta t}(x, y) = d_t(x, y) + \frac{\Delta t \left(\sum f_{\text{in}} - \sum f_{\text{out}}\right)}{l_x l_y} - \text{LeakRate} \cdot d_t(x, y) + \text{SplashInput}(x, y)$$

#### Material Integration:
The resulting height map generates a dynamic water normal:
$$\mathbf{N}_{\text{water}} = \text{normalize}\left(-\frac{\partial (b + d)}{\partial x}, 1.0, -\frac{\partial (b + d)}{\partial z}\right)$$
The final pixel shader lerps between dry wood parameters and wet water parameters:
$$\mathbf{N}_{\text{final}} = \text{lerp}(\mathbf{N}_{\text{wood}}, \mathbf{N}_{\text{water}}, \text{saturate}(d \cdot 10.0))$$
$$\text{Roughness} = \text{lerp}(\text{Roughness}_{\text{wood}}, 0.05, \text{saturate}(d \cdot 20.0))$$

## 4. Deep Dive 3: Volumetric Stylized Cloud Rendering & G-Buffer Packing

### The Challenge of Stylized Volumetric Clouds
Traditional AAA volumetric clouds (such as Andrew Schneider's Guerrilla Games system for *Horizon Zero Dawn*) use full-screen raymarching through 3D Perlin-Worley noise textures. While photorealistic, Rare encountered two obstacles:
1. **Performance Budget:** Fullscreen raymarching is too expensive across low-spec hardware and Xbox One when combined with the dynamic ocean.
2. **Stylistic Target:** Procedural noise produces realistic puffy cumulus formations, but fails to match the sculptural, brush-stroke shapes established by Art Director Ryan Stevenson.

### Rare's Hybrid Proxy Pipeline

```
[Sculpted Low-Poly Proxy Mesh] 
           │
           ▼
[Forward Render Pass: Per-Vertex Lit Off-Screen Target]
           │
           ▼
[Quarter-Resolution Downsample (1/4 Width, 1/4 Height)]
           │
           ▼
[Single-Pass Compute Shader: Depth-Dependent Gaussian Blur]
           │
           ▼
[G-Buffer Texture Packing (Sunlight in R, Skylight in G, Depth/Alpha in B/A)]
           │
           ▼
[Full-Screen Composite with Fog, Depth Testing & Spyglass FOV]
```

### Depth-Dependent Compute Gaussian Blur
To convert faceted low-poly meshes into soft, voluminous clouds, Rare implemented a compute shader blur whose kernel radius / standard deviation $\sigma$ depends dynamically on scene depth $z$:
$$\sigma(z) = \sigma_0 \cdot \left(\frac{z - z_{\text{near}}}{z_{\text{far}} - z_{\text{near}}}\right)^p$$
- Nearby clouds receive a wider pixel radius blur to eliminate faceted polygon edges.
- Distant horizon clouds receive a tighter blur to retain crisp painterly silhouettes without bleeding into the sea.

### Packing the G-Buffer Target
Because of memory and bandwidth constraints, the entire cloud lighting and geometry state is compressed into a single 4-channel texture:
- **Red (R):** Direct Sunlight intensity (calculated via directional extinction).
- **Green (G):** Ambient Skylight intensity (calculated via upper/lower spherical harmonics).
- **Blue (B):** Blurred scene depth (used for fog blending and camera clipping).
- **Alpha (A):** Cloud opacity / coverage.

### Light Scattering Model
Rather than a full multiple-scattering simulation, Rare used a modified forward-scattering phase function:
$$P(\theta) = \frac{1 - g^2}{(1 + g^2 - 2g \cos\theta)^{3/2}} + \text{Powder}(d)$$
$$\text{Powder}(d) = 1.0 - \exp(-2.0 \cdot d)$$
The powder term simulates dark rims around cloud edges where light fails to scatter directly back to the eye, giving clouds internal density and sculptural mass.

## 5. Deep Dive 4: Ship Ropes & Rigging via Verlet Integration (Jakobsen 2001)

### Why PhysX Constraints Were Rejected
A galleon in *Sea of Thieves* has dozens of ropes, pulleys, rigging lines, and sails that sway with ship roll and player steering. Using standard rigid-body physics joints (PhysX 6-DOF constraints):
- Causes severe numerical instability ("jitter" or "explosions") when high forces are applied.
- Exceeds the CPU physics budget when multiple crew ships engage in battle.

### Thomas Jakobsen's Verlet Formulation
Rare implemented particle-based cables updated using Thomas Jakobsen's *Advanced Character Physics* (GDC 2001) numerical scheme:

$$\mathbf{x}_{n+1} = 2\mathbf{x}_n - \mathbf{x}_{n-1} + \mathbf{a} \cdot \Delta t^2$$

Where:
- $\mathbf{x}_n$: Current position of the rope node.
- $\mathbf{x}_{n-1}$: Previous position of the rope node (velocity is implicitly stored as $\mathbf{x}_n - \mathbf{x}_{n-1}$).
- $\mathbf{a}$: Acceleration (gravity, wind impulse, and ship deck inertial forces).

#### Distance Relaxation Constraint
For each segment between node $i$ and node $i+1$ with rest length $L_0$:
$$\mathbf{\Delta} = \mathbf{x}_{i+1} - \mathbf{x}_i$$
$$\text{distance} = |\mathbf{\Delta}|$$
$$\text{difference} = \frac{\text{distance} - L_0}{\text{distance}}$$
$$\mathbf{x}_i \mathrel{+}= 0.5 \cdot \mathbf{\Delta} \cdot \text{difference}$$
$$\mathbf{x}_{i+1} \mathrel{-}= 0.5 \cdot \mathbf{\Delta} \cdot \text{difference}$$

Running 3 to 5 iterations of this relaxation per frame guarantees that ropes never stretch or penetrate the mast.

#### The "Infinite Reel" & Pulley Solution
To handle ropes winding through multiple pulleys when raising sails or anchors:
1. One end of the rope is designated as the **Anchor** (fixed to an animated prop).
2. The opposite end is an **Infinite Reel** (dynamically adjusting the total rest length $L_{\text{total}}$).
3. If precision breaks down as rope nodes compress closely together, the algorithm automatically collapses the intermediate segments into a straight-line vector, preserving visual continuity without physics singularities.

## 6. Deep Dive 5: The Kraken: Pixar's "Hank" FEM to Vertex Animation Textures (VAT)

### The Production Dilemma
The Kraken requires multiple massive tentacles to wrap tightly around irregular galleon hulls, smash decks, and react to cannon fire.
- **CPU Skeletal Rigging:** Evaluating complex multi-bone inverse kinematics (IK) on the CPU for 8+ tentacles with dozens of skinning joints per tentacle exceeded the console frame budget.
- **Mesh Penetration:** Standard linear blend skinning caused tentacles to collapse volume or intersect directly through ship geometry.

### Pixar's "Hank" Reference (*Finding Dory*, SIGGRAPH 2016)
In the SIGGRAPH 2016 talk *"Finding Hank: Or How to Sim an Octopus"*, Pixar solved octopus simulation by coupling animator-keyed guide curves with a **Finite Element Method (FEM)** tetrahedral soft-tissue solver in Houdini, ensuring fleshy volume preservation and sucker deformation.

### Rare's Real-Time GPU Implementation Pipeline

```
[Animator Posed Guide Curves] 
           │
           ▼
[Houdini Finite Element Method (FEM) Soft-Tissue Simulation]
           │
           ▼
[Non-Penetrating Wrap Solvers Against Galleon Hull Collision Proxy]
           │
           ▼
[SideFX Labs Vertex Animation Textures (VAT) Exporter]
  ├── Texture A (16-bit Float RGB): XYZ World Position Offsets
  └── Texture B (8-bit RGBA): Surface Normals (RGB) + Tangent Mask (A)
           │
           ▼
[Unreal Engine 4 Vertex Shader: 100% GPU Deformed Playback (Zero CPU Cost)]
```

### Frame Reduction & Compression
- The original Houdini simulation spanned **900 frames**.
- Rare sampled down to **117 keyframes**, storing only key extreme deformation states.
- The runtime vertex shader performs smooth linear interpolation between adjacent sub-frames:
  $$\mathbf{P}_{\text{final}} = \text{lerp}\left(\text{SampleVAT}(t_{\text{prev}}), \text{SampleVAT}(t_{\text{next}}), \text{frac}(t)\right)$$

### Comparison: VAT vs. Naughty Dog's Skinned Mesh Reduction
In the talk, Valentine Kozin references Naughty Dog's technique (from *Uncharted 4*):
- **Naughty Dog Approach:** Convert dense vertex animations into an arbitrary low-count bone hierarchy via principal component analysis (PCA) / skinning decomposition.
- **Why Rare Chose VAT:** While Naughty Dog's approach saves texture memory, it still requires CPU skin matrix evaluation. Because *Sea of Thieves* was heavily CPU-bound on Xbox One due to multiplayer networking, physics, and AI, shifting 100% of tentacle evaluation to the GPU vertex pipeline via VAT was the optimal trade-off.

## 7. Deep Dive 6: Procedural Branching Lightning & Atmospheric Scatter

### Algorithmic Breakdown
The lightning system in *Sea of Thieves* generates non-repeating procedural electrical discharges during tropical squalls:

1. **Midpoint Displacement with Directional Bias:**
   - Starts with a root strike point in the storm cloud $\mathbf{P}_{\text{cloud}}$ and target point $\mathbf{P}_{\text{strike}}$ (ocean or mast).
   - Iteratively subdivides line segments, adding perpendicular displacement:
     $$\mathbf{P}_{\text{mid}} = \frac{\mathbf{P}_1 + \mathbf{P}_2}{2} + \text{random\_vector} \cdot \text{scale} \cdot |\mathbf{P}_2 - \mathbf{P}_1|$$
2. **Branching Probability:**
   - At each subdivision step, there is a $20\% - 35\%$ chance to spawn a secondary daughter branch with reduced thickness and shorter length.
3. **Synchronized Atmospheric Flash:**
   - A single-frame high-intensity point light is placed at the strike coordinates.
   - The directional light in the cloud off-screen buffer is temporarily multiplied by a flash scalar, illuminating the cloud G-buffer from within.
   - The ocean surface shader samples this flash term, producing an instant specular burst across the water surface aligned with the lightning bolt position.

## Summary of Key Takeaways for Technical Artists

1. **Stylization Requires Physical Grounding:** Rather than inventing non-physical shader hacks, start with physical models (Tessendorf FFT, shallow-water Navier-Stokes, FEM elasticity) and apply artistic filtering to control frequency, contrast, and color palette.
2. **Offload the CPU via Vertex Animation Textures (VAT):** Complex organic deformations (tentacles, cloth, foliage) can be simulated offline in Houdini and evaluated in real-time on the GPU vertex stage with negligible performance impact.
3. **Filter Geometry with Depth-Aware Compute Passes:** Solid proxy meshes can be transformed into volumetric appearances using screen-space compute shaders whose kernels scale with depth, saving milliseconds compared to 3D volumetric raymarching.
4. **Deterministic Math for Multiplayer Physics:** When simulating water in networked multiplayer games, compute water height analytically on both client CPU and GPU to ensure perfect synchrony without network replication overhead.
